# Effect of Annealing Temperature on Microstructure and Mechanical Properties of a High-Nitrogen Austenitic Stainless Steel

## Abstract

High-nitrogen austenitic stainless steels are promising non-magnetic structural
materials. The design target of this alloy family is a yield strength above
600 MPa combined with good ductility. In this work, two steels were investigated
and the effect of solution annealing temperature was studied.

## 1. Introduction

Conventional 304/316 austenitic stainless steels have limited strength. Adding
nitrogen is an effective way to strengthen the austenite matrix. Previous studies
reported yield strengths in the range of 400 to 900 MPa depending on nitrogen
content and grain size. The present study focuses on a Cr-Mn-N steel (Steel A)
and a reference Cr-Ni steel (Steel B).

## 2. Materials and Methods

Two steels were prepared by vacuum induction melting, hot forging and hot rolling
into 12 mm plates.

Table 1. Chemical composition (wt.%).

| Steel | C | Cr | Mn | Ni | N |
|-------|------|------|------|------|------|
| Steel A (Cr-Mn-N) | 0.05 | 18.2 | 10.5 | 0.3 | 0.52 |
| Steel B (Cr-Ni ref.) | 0.04 | 18.0 | 1.2 | 10.4 | 0.03 |

Steel A plates were solution annealed at 700, 720 and 740 °C for 30 min followed
by water quenching. Steel B was solution annealed at 1050 °C for 30 min as a
reference condition.

Tensile tests were carried out at room temperature. Microstructures were observed
by optical microscopy (OM), scanning electron microscopy (SEM), and phase
identification was done by X-ray diffraction (XRD).

## 3. Results

### 3.1 Mechanical properties

Table 2. Room-temperature tensile properties of Steel A.

| Condition | Annealing (°C) | Yield strength (MPa) | Tensile strength (MPa) | Elongation (%) |
|-----------|----------------|----------------------|------------------------|----------------|
| A-700 | 700 | 685 | 1020 | 42 |
| A-720 | 720 | 660 | 995 | 45 |
| A-740 | 740 | 632 | 970 | 48 |

For the reference Steel B, the measured yield strength was 245 MPa and the
tensile strength was 585 MPa at room temperature.

## 4. Conclusions

The Cr-Mn-N steel (Steel A) reaches a yield strength of 632 to 685 MPa depending
on the annealing temperature, exceeding the 600 MPa target, while remaining fully
austenitic and non-magnetic.
